# Jakob: transferable expectations

## Mechanism and boundary

People interpret a new interface using expectations learned elsewhere. Familiar
relationships between a control, its label, its location, and its consequence
reduce the learning needed before the task. A mental model describes what a
person believes the system does; it can differ from the implementation and from
the designer's model.

Familiarity concerns behavior and meaning, not a requirement to copy a competitor
or make every product look alike. Relevant conventions depend on the audience,
platform, domain, and experience. A common pattern can still be harmful or poorly
suited to this task.

## Diagnose and act

- Trace the task in the existing design system and adjacent familiar products.
  Identify the expectation a user brings: where to search, how selection works,
  whether a control saves immediately, what Back restores.
- Compare expected and actual behavior. Preserve meaningful mappings and give
  novel controls clear signifiers. A style change can differentiate the brand
  without changing interaction semantics.
- For a redesign, inventory learned locations, terminology, shortcuts, saved
  work, and repeated routines. Stage high-impact changes where practical, show
  a contextual explanation, and consider a bounded preview/reversion period
  when maintaining two versions is feasible. A rollout strategy is a product
  choice, not a universal obligation.
- Prefer guidance at the point of use to a tutorial that asks people to memorize
  the entire interface. Permit learning by doing with recoverable actions.

Personas are useful when grounded in aggregated research about goals, behavior,
constraints, and knowledge. Treat a fabricated persona as an assumption, and
avoid demographic details that have no bearing on the decision. Ask actual users
to explain what they think a control will do before revealing its result.

## Verify

Compare first-use comprehension and task success with repeat-use performance.
For a redesign, include returning users and real shortcuts, not only new users.
Track wrong turns, assistance, and recovery as well as completion time. A novelty
that consistently improves the task can justify departing from convention.

## Original example

An inventory app renames the familiar “Stock movements” section to “Activity
studio.” Operators cannot predict whether it contains receipts or audit logs.
Restore the domain label, retain a distinctive visual treatment, and test finding
a recent receipt. This addresses the mapping problem without prescribing a
generic visual identity.
