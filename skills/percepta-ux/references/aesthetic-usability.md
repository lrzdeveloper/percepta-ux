# Aesthetic-Usability: appeal and task performance

## Mechanism and boundary

Visual appeal can shape first impressions, perceived ease, and tolerance of minor
problems. Fast, automatic judgments and slower, deliberate reasoning both matter
to interaction. Treat the two-system account as an explanatory model, not a way
to identify a user's brain activity from a screen.

Attractiveness, actual usability, credibility, and preference are distinct outcomes.
Their relationship depends on the interface, person, task, and context. A beautiful
screen is neither proof of task success nor evidence that a service deserves trust.
Avoid presenting the book's reported first-impression timing as a universal
deadline or promising a fixed cognitive or commercial benefit.

## Diagnose and act

- Improve craft where it clarifies the work: coherent type hierarchy, spacing,
  alignment, understandable controls, intentional imagery, legible copy, and
  consistent states. Retain the product's visual direction and existing tokens.
- Make perceived ease compatible with actual access and behavior. Subtle low
  contrast, hidden controls, motion, and missing status can undermine the task
  even when the page looks refined.
- Evaluate polish separately from structure. When comparing interaction variants,
  control visual fidelity as far as practical so appearance does not dominate
  the comparison. A polished prototype and a rough one can test different things.
- During usability studies, observe successful completion, errors, hesitation,
  backtracking, and help requests before interpreting praise as evidence. Ask
  about specific behavior and consequences without leading people toward the
  desired aesthetic judgment.
- Preserve criticism even when participants rationalize a failure after enjoying
  the visual design. Their positive response and their difficulty can both be real.

For detailed study design, use [Research methods](research-methods.md). Pair visual
review with runtime evidence where the claim concerns behavior. A source change
or screenshot alone does not demonstrate accessibility conformance.

## Verify

Report task success, time/effort, and errors alongside perceived ease or preference.
Keep sample size, task, prototype limitations, and uncertainty visible. Small
formative studies identify problems; they do not justify precise population
estimates or a predicted conversion lift. Recheck the task after visual changes
that affect hierarchy or discoverability.

## Original example

People praise the photography in a booking flow but repeatedly choose the wrong
date. Preserve its visual identity while clarifying selected and unavailable dates,
labels, and the review step. Ask participants to book a specific date range and
separately collect their impressions. Visual approval does not close the defect.
