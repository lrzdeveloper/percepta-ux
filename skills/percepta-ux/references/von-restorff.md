# Von Restorff: meaningful distinction

## Mechanism and boundary

An item that differs from surrounding items can be more memorable. Contrast can
also attract attention, but being seen, being understood, being remembered, and
being chosen are different outcomes. Selective attention depends on the task;
even a highly visible element can be ignored when it resembles an advertisement.

Emphasis is relative. If every card or action competes to be primary, distinction
disappears. Change blindness means a dynamic update may be missed when attention
is elsewhere; merely changing a color does not guarantee awareness.

## Diagnose and act

- Identify what should receive attention in this state and why it serves the
  person's task. Give consequential changes or decisions proportionate emphasis.
- Use a controlled hierarchy of size, position, spacing, shape, weight, and color.
  Preserve meaning across states and components. Remove competing emphasis
  before making the important element louder.
- Inspect whether legitimate information looks like an ad or appears in a region
  people tend to ignore. Bring actionable status closer to its affected object.
- Pair color with explicit text, icons/shapes, or patterns that communicate the
  actual meaning. Provide adequate contrast, visible focus, accessible names,
  and semantic status communication as appropriate.
- Use motion deliberately, honor reduced-motion preferences, and provide a
  nonmoving equivalent. Do not require a transient animation to understand an
  outcome. Avoid flashing or competing attention capture.
- Distinguish a destructive action without making accidental activation easier.
  The action can be clear while the safe recovery path remains available;
  emphasis, default focus, and confirmation policy are separate choices.

Use [Standards](standards.md) for exact conformance claims and [Ethics](ethics.md)
when prominence promotes purchase, permission, engagement, or commitment.
Eye tracking can study gaze allocation when equipment and a suitable question
exist; a heatmap does not establish comprehension, emotion, or consent.

## Verify

Give the user a relevant task, then inspect whether they notice and correctly
interpret the important content. Test in context, including neighboring emphasis,
dynamic updates, keyboard access, and reduced motion. A recall question or
attention study answers a different question from successful task execution.

## Original example

An approval dashboard makes every status a bright badge and highlights a paid
upgrade above a blocked invoice. Quiet routine metadata and give the actual
blocker an explicit label and contextual resolution action. Verify that people
identify why the invoice cannot proceed, not just which badge they remember.
