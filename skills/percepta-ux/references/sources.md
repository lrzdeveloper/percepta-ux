# Sources and coverage

## Primary conceptual source

Jon Yablonski, *Laws of UX: Using Psychology to Design Better Products & Services*,
second edition, O'Reilly, 2024. Print ISBN 978-1-098-14696-2.
[Publisher record](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/laws-of-ux/9781098146955/).
[Author's book page](https://lawsofux.com/book/).

References use the book's printed pagination. Percepta contains original
operational guidance and examples. It is independent of the author and publisher;
their book, text, and illustrations remain under their respective rights.

## Complementary public resource

[Laws of UX](https://lawsofux.com/) is the author's broader public collection.
It includes concepts beyond the ten principal chapter subjects in this edition.
Do not describe every item on the website as a separate chapter of this book.
Consult a relevant primary source if a task requires a principle not covered here.

The current W3C source links, version/level distinctions and exceptions are
maintained in [Standards](standards.md).

## Practical extension boundary

Percepta adds the [command workflows](../SKILL.md#choose-a-workflow),
[visual craft](visual-craft.md), [component contracts](components.md),
[motion guidance](motion.md), [reference selection](reference-selection.md),
[interaction patterns](interaction-patterns.md), [product context](product-context.md),
[UX copy](ux-copy.md), and [review/verification](review-loop.md), plus the optional
contrast helper documented under [Standards](standards.md). Shared research
decisions link to the full [method protocols](research-protocols.md). Local context-loading and draft-count helpers support
execution without changing the source attribution. These original additions
support execution and selection; they are not extra book chapters or redistributed
third-party skills.

Evidence labels, prioritization, decision records, transaction-state and retry
discipline, explicit AI verification, uncertainty-aware eye-tracking interpretation,
current WCAG distinctions, evaluation cases, and all original scenarios are
Percepta's adaptations. They should not be attributed to the book as direct claims.
The source's historical percentages, broad behavioral claims, product examples,
and numerical rules are not imported as universal evidence for a new product.
